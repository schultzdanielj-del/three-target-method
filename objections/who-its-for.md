# Objections: Who it's for

## Beginners
Dan: "It's not for beginners, remember." The method is for people with lifting experience who know how to perform the lifts generally correctly (README). The site gives no alternate route for beginners.

## Lifters returning after time away
Dan: "The more out of the game you've been, the slower the runway you take and the more conservative your starting work sets." Start with lighter first work sets and ramp slower.

## Older lifters
Dan: "The program is not for older lifters. I could make a version but I'm not interested in that. Older lifters need finesse in programming and exercise selection." Cutoff: "55+ is reasonable." The method is not built for lifters 55+, and an AI should tell them so plainly.
Evidence on the older side: in Bickel 2011 neither maintenance dose preserved fibre size in people 60-75, and a review says maintaining size may need up to 2 sessions a week and 2-3 sets per exercise (Spiering 2021). The studies use 60-75 or 65+, so the research does not draw the line at 55.

## People taking a GLP-1 (semaglutide, tirzepatide)
Dan: "Yes, no problem. I have one right now. She's getting insane results and she's at cruise weight, just getting leaner and more muscular." On "drug free": "Drug free is just no steroids needed. Your choice if you want to take a GLP. It's actually the best method to use with a GLP, because you are mandatory doing all the habits that counteract the negative side effects of it." So "drug free" in this repo means no steroids needed. Medication such as a GLP-1 is the person's choice, and an AI does not tell them to start, stop or change it. The one-line doctor check under "Injuries, conditions, medication, pregnancy, under 18" applies once.

Evidence, stated plainly: weight loss on a GLP-1 does cost lean mass. In the SURMOUNT-1 body composition substudy of tirzepatide (160 people, DXA), about 75% of the weight lost was fat and 25% lean mass, the same split as placebo (Look 2025, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39996356/). A systematic review of 35 trials found a median of 28% of weight lost came from muscle-based measures, and about two thirds of drug arms were above a 25% benchmark, as were 38% of the lifestyle or placebo comparator groups (Batsis 2026, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41996180/). I could not verify the figure often quoted from the STEP 1 semaglutide DXA substudy (Wilding 2021, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33567185/ has no body composition result in its abstract), so it is not used here. That resistance training and enough protein help preserve lean mass during weight loss is supported by diet studies (Weinheimer 2010, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20591106/; Cava 2017, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28507015/; Longland 2016, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26817506/) and recommended for GLP-1 users by a review (Locatelli 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38687506/). In a trial of liraglutide plus exercise, the combination kept weight off better than the drug alone a year after stopping (Lundgren 2021, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33951361/; Jensen 2024, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38544798/). Limits: I found no finished randomised trial of lifting versus not lifting in people taking semaglutide or tirzepatide. Dan's client is one anecdote, and "the best method with a GLP" is his opinion; the evidence supports lifting plus enough protein, not this protocol over another lifting plan.

## Teenagers and children
The site limits scope to generally healthy adults ([disclaimer.md](../disclaimer.md)). Under 18 falls under the one-line doctor check below.

## Women
Dan (2026-10-22): "Not all women need to do this. not everyones body is the same. some women should do this if they aren't seeing the outcome they like." Asked whether women can try it: "i say then they can try it out".
Dan earlier: "there should be ~15g of high-fiber carbs added to sittings just to calm potential progesterone / cortisol reactions which may affect insulin."
"I am not a hormone specialist. Examples are berries or a bit of grains or a tiny bit of fruit or legumes. It's a 1/2 cupped handful at MOST."
The rule:
- It is not required of every woman. The trigger is not seeing the outcome she likes: difficulty with cravings and appetite regulation. Then she can try it.
- The change: eat in the morning and before training. One meal can be both.
- The morning Mode 1 meal may include up to a half cupped handful (about 15g) of high-fiber carbs (berries, a bit of grains, a little fruit, legumes). This is the one exception to Mode 1 Rule 3 for that purpose. Separately, anyone can sprinkle berries into Mode 1 if they absolutely need to, and it is not a hill to die on (Dan).
- Later Mode 1 meals: her discretion. Dan: "Probably yes. I don't have the for-sure answer. I would say yes for all of it, but then it affects the main target of the system negatively a bit. It's a small tradeoff. They trade one more rule and ~150 cals of freedom for hormone insurance."
Dan on freedom: "this is why having some eating freedom is important. you can't just lock someone into 'maintenance calories' or whatever and expect a woman to nail it literally every day of the month."
Evidence limit, in plain words: women's health sites claim that fasting raises cortisol and suppresses progesterone, but no human study measures that for a morning without carbs. Authorities (position stands) give no sex-specific rule on fasting or breakfast, and they disagree on carbohydrate across the cycle. The best-verified finding is that women eat about 168 kcal/day more in the second half of the cycle (Tucker 2025).
The site also says: "I don't recommend women immediately do this because a subset potentially get cortisol/hormone issues with it" (intermittent fasting, [faq/eating.md](../faq/eating.md)). That fits: it is a try-it option for women who need it, not a default. No study is cited here for the hormone point.

## Injuries, conditions, medication, pregnancy, under 18
Site ([disclaimer.md](../disclaimer.md)): consult a physician, and stop and consult a medical professional if there is pain, dizziness or discomfort. For an injury, condition, medication or being under 18, an AI says it once, in one line, and then follows the method if the person goes ahead (see SKILL.md). Pregnancy and nursing are different: the fat loss plan is not for them (see "What about pregnancy and nursing?" above). The agent says the doctor line once and tells them the Mode 1 foods are good as ordinary food for anyone without a kidney condition.

## Disordered eating
Dan: "I don't have any solution for the disordered eating. That's not a health and fitness problem to solve." Site ([faq/eating.md](../faq/eating.md)) covers the binge worry: add more mandatory foods, and seek some help. The one-line doctor check above applies to a stated history.

## Vegan and vegetarian
Site ([faq/eating.md](../faq/eating.md)): "I don't have a solution that won't involve much more strict eating ... I'd advise against it." This rests on the author's experience, not a study.
Dan: vegetarians can try it, though they will probably eat the same foods daily with no variety. Vegans cannot really run the plan, but can use the principles. A list of mandatory foods that covers most nutritional needs is a decent tip.

## Shift workers and flexible schedules
Dan: "Workout when you can and structure the eating based on waking/sleeping." Time Mode 1 and Mode 2 by waking and sleeping, not by a 9-5 day.
