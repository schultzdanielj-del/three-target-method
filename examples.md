# Worked examples

These are base plans, built from real plans, with no names. Every reason below is Dan's own, taken from his answers. Where Dan gave no reason for a choice, the plan is shown and nothing is claimed about it. Section numbers refer to [programming.md](programming.md). Exercise names only are shown, in the order they appear in the plan.

## Example A (coach)

Profile: man, adjustable dumbbells up to 90 lb, narrow hip structure and narrow waist. 4 letters.

Plan:
- A: Roman Chair Side Hold, Roman Chair Situps, Low Incline DB Bench, Decline DB Bench, Seated DB Shoulder Press, Seated DB Lateral Raises
- B: Chest Supported DB Rows, Pulldowns, Single Arm DB Rows, Reverse Hypers
- C: Single Leg Calf Raises, Alternating DB Hammer Curl, Standing DB Curls, Lying DB Triceps Extensions, Straight Bar Pushdown
- D: Front Loaded Barbell Reverse Lunges, Glute Ham Raises, Dumbbell Sissy Squat, Barbell Hip Thrusts

Why:
- Decline DB bench is the second chest movement instead of a fly, because a fly is easy to cheat (section 8, Chest; section 11).
- Roman chair side holds are in this plan because of a very narrow hip structure and narrow waist: added oblique muscle gives a good shape. That is not the case for every individual (section 8, Abs).
- The seated DB press comes after the bench presses. Never vertical press before horizontal, it feels terrible. Horizontal then vertical is fine (section 11).
- Reverse hypers are last on the back letter. Hinges go near the end, so the spine and core are still fresh and less weight is needed (section 8, Hinges).
- Calves sit on an upper body letter. You cannot train calves first and then do lunges and squats on cooked calves (section 10).
- On the leg letter the lunges are first and the sissy squat is last. The knees need to be warmed up, and being pre-fatigued means less load, so the knees do not blow up (section 11).
- The leg letter has 4 exercises, because legs are more taxing on the body (section 2).

## Example B (woman, glute and hamstring dominant goals)

Profile: woman, higher skilled, more training experience, female physique goals (more emphasis on lower body, shoulders and back). Glute and hamstring dominant goals. Newer to the coach than to training. 4 letters.

Plan:
- A: Bulgarian Split Squat, Pendulum Squat, Captains Chair Knee Raise, Plank
- B: High Incline Dumbbell Bench Press, Assisted Dip Machine, Incline Dumbbell Tricep Extension, Standing Dumbbell Lateral Raise, Incline Dumbbell Y Raise
- C: Machine Seated Leg Curl, 45 Degree Back Raise, Barbell Hip Thrust, Machine Hip Abduction, Toe Press
- D: Assisted Chinup Machine, Dumbbell Single Arm Row, Seated Cable Row, Rope Facepull, Seated Dumbbell Curl

Why:
- The split is not push, pull, legs. Her letters carry more lower body, shoulders and back. Legs are spread over two letters (A and C) and the arm work is folded into the push and pull letters (section 2).
- Glutes, lateral hip and hamstrings are split across letters so they are hit fresher and harder (section 7).
- Letter A has two quad movements: one single leg movement and one deep knee bend movement (section 7, section 8 Quads).
- Letter C has four glute and hamstring movements. Glutes and hamstrings are two separate muscle groups, with 2 direct movements each, and her goals are glute and hamstring dominant, so the maximum slots are filled (section 7).
- Calves (the toe press) sit on a leg letter here, because her legs are split across two days, so calves can go on one of them (section 10).
- The high incline press fills the vertical press slot (section 8, Shoulders).
- The Y raise is the second shoulder movement. It is a cheap way to get some lateral delt work while building shoulder mobility and scapular stability (section 8, Shoulders).
- Dips count as chest and triceps, and can count as the second triceps movement. People start on the assisted dip machine (section 8, Biceps and triceps).
- The back letter has a face pull and no rear delt fly. New clients nearly always have weak upper backs and need a good chunk of time on external rotations, lower traps, serratus and scapular tilt movements. The rear delt fly is a heavy duty movement. The plan is to top out the face pull (can no longer stand anchored against the weight), beat it as a confidence booster, then upgrade to the rear delt fly (section 8, Back).

## Example C (woman, trains at home and at a commercial gym)

Profile: woman with a home setup and access to a commercial gym. Poor overhead mobility (first rib and thoracic spine restrictions). Good lifter. 4 letters. Home option shown below. Every slot has a second, gym option for the same muscle (for example Machine Chest Press, Pec Deck, Assisted Chinup Machine, Chest Supported Row Machine, Reverse Pec Deck, Machine Seated Leg Curl).

Plan (home option):
- A: Low Incline Dumbbell Bench Press, High Incline Dumbbell Bench Press, Seated Lateral Raises, Standing Lateral Raises, Single Leg Calf Raises
- B: Banded Pullups, Inverted Rows, Barbell Rows, Head Supported Rear Delt Flyes, Chest Supported Dumbbell Rows
- C: Ab Wheel Rollouts, Side Planks, Alternating Dumbbell Curls, Barbell Curls, Barbell Skullcrusher, Incline Dumbbell Triceps Extensions
- D: ATG Split Squats, Ball Hamstring Curls, Romanian Deadlifts, Barbell Hip Thrust

Why:
- Each slot has a home option and a commercial gym option, with the plan toggling between them (introduction of programming.md).
- Two lateral raise movements and a high incline press, with no overhead press, because of the poor overhead mobility (section 8, Shoulders; section 17).
- Barbell Rows are in the home version because of an equipment limitation. A barbell row near the end of the workout is acceptable when there is no other row option and the person is not yet strong enough to run into problems (section 8, Other).
- A Romanian deadlift is here despite the usual leaving out of Romanian deadlifts, because she is a good lifter whose technique the coach knows well, and it is a lighter, fixed reps and sets movement (section 8, Hinges; section 17).
- The leg letter has 4 exercises, because legs are more taxing on the body (section 2).

## Example D (man, neck nerve problem)

Profile: man. A nerve impingement in the neck affects certain movements: lateral raises hurt it, Y raises do not. 4 letters.

Plan:
- A: Dumbbell Weighted Situps, Weighted Planks, Low Incline Dumbbell Bench Press, DB Floor Fly, High Incline Dumbbell Bench Press, Incline DB Y Raise
- B: Dumbbell Chest Supported Row, Lat Pulldown, Single Arm Dumbbell Row, Dumbbell Kelso Shrug
- C: Dumbbell Alternating Hammer Curl, Seated Underhand Grip Bicep Curl, Incline EZ Bar Skullcrushers, Single Leg Calf Raises, Side Lying External Rotations
- D: Front Loaded Barbell Reverse Lunge, Nordic Ham Curl, Sissy Squat, Barbell Hip Thrusts

Why:
- Letter A looks like it has three chest movements, but a high incline press is not really a chest movement. It is a vertical press (section 8, Chest).
- A Y raise replaces the lateral raise, because lateral raises hurt the neck nerve and Y raises do not (section 8, Shoulders).
- Nordic ham curls are not an ideal movement. They are used only when there is no machine, glute ham raise or ball, and only for someone already really strong. He does the plan and is not overweight (section 8, Glutes and hamstrings).
- On the leg letter the sissy squat is last (section 11).

## Example E (man, bad shoulder and elbow tendonitis)

Profile: man with the weakest upper back of the examples here, a bad shoulder, elbow tendonitis, low back issues, and weak glutes and hips with poor lockout technique. 4 letters.

Plan:
- A: Incline Barbell Bench Press, Flat DB Bench Press, Seated Lateral Raises, Lateral Raises, Weighted Planks
- B: DB Chest Supported Rows, Pulldowns, Seated Low Cable Rows, Single Arm DB Rows, Head Supported Rear Delt Flys
- C: Toe Press, Seated Dumbbell Curls, Cable Straight Bar Bicep Curl, Incline DB Triceps Extensions, Straight Bar Pushdowns
- D: Dumbbell Reverse Lunges, Leg Press Machine, Lying Machine Leg Curl, 45 Degree Back Raise

Why:
- The back letter has 5 back movements, not 4, because he has the weakest upper back and it goes together with overdone chest and biceps, plus the bad shoulder and elbow tendonitis (section 8, Back).
- The back raise is there because he has no reverse hyper. Light back raises with proper technique get the hips moving more and the low back moving less. They are light for accuracy (section 8, Hinges).
- The lunges and the back raise are light sets (fixed sets and reps: 2x12-15 per side and 3x12-20). His low back issues, weak glutes and hips and poor lockout technique mean he needs lots of glute lockout reps (section 8, Glutes and hamstrings).

## Example F (woman, commercial gym)

Profile: woman who trains in a commercial gym. Shoulder issues: poor scapular stability, no low traps, no scapular tilt, serratus or first rib mobility. 4 letters. Four slots have a second option for the same muscle (shown as the first option below): Incline Push Ups for the low incline press, Bent Over Rear Delt Fly for the Reverse Pec Deck, Captains Chair Knee Raise for the situp, Romanian Deadlift for the back raise.

Plan:
- A: Calf Raise Machine, Machine Chest Press, Low Incline DB Bench, Dumbbell Lateral Raises, Side Lying External Rotations
- B: Assisted Neutral Grip Pullups, Hammer Strength Chest Supported Row, Lat Pulldowns, Single Arm DB Rows, Reverse Pec Deck
- C: Decline Bench Situps, Side Plank, Incline DB Triceps Extensions, Straight Bar Pushdowns, Seated Dumbbell Curls
- D: Bulgarian Split Squats, Leg Press, 45 Degree Back Raises, Machine Seated Leg Curls

Why:
- The second options on four slots are because the gym is crowded (introduction of programming.md).
- Calves are first on the letter so she does not skip them (section 8, Other).
- Two chest presses, lateral raises and side lying external rotations, with no vertical press, because of her shoulder issues. Poor scapular stability, no low traps, no tilt ability or serratus, a jacked up first rib. 80% or more of people have this problem (section 8, Shoulders; section 23).
- The side lying external rotation is a remedial exercise that can be added instead of a second shoulder slot when someone has general shoulder issues (section 8, Shoulders).

## Example G (woman, home, TRX, no pullup bar, no barbell)

Profile: woman training at home with dumbbells, a bench, a suspension trainer (TRX) and bands. No barbell and no pullup bar. 3 letters.

Plan:
- A: Rear Foot Elevated Split Squat, Sissy Squats, Assisted Single Leg Deadlift, TRX Fallouts, TRX Anti Rotation Press
- B: Incline DB Bench Press, Flat Dumbbell Flys, Single Arm DB Shoulder Press, TRX Triceps Extensions, Seated Lateral Raises
- C: Dumbbell Chest Supported Rows, Dumbbell Single Arm Rows, Seated Band Pulldowns, TRX High Rows, Seated Dumbbell Curls

Why:
- Legs and abs share letter A. It is the only way to fit it in for her (section 2).
- An assisted single leg deadlift replaces the hip thrust and leg curl: she is not strong enough for TRX leg curls, and there is no barbell for a hip thrust (section 8, Glutes and hamstrings).
- Band pulldowns fill the vertical pull because she will never get a pullup bar. It is a poor substitute that Dan accepts only in this case (section 8, Back).
- One triceps movement while chest has two: adding a second triceps movement and taking out the fly is a wash, so either is acceptable on a short letter (section 7).
- A suspension trainer is a better choice than a few dumbbells or bands, because progress can be graded inch by inch. The strap length and where the person stands must be tracked accurately for each exercise (introduction of programming.md).

## Example H (woman, the smallest plan)

Profile: woman, assumed to have female physique goals (a heuristic, until you get to know her more). The plan is the least possible sacrifice package. 3 letters.

Plan:
- A: Stir the Pot, Close Grip Bench Press, Incline DB Bench Press, Seated Lateral Raises, Flat DB Triceps Extensions
- B: Band Chinups, Single Arm Pulldowns, Single Arm DB Rows, Chest Supported Rear Delt Flyes, Seated Underhand Grip DB Curls
- C: DB Reverse Lunges, VMO Goblet Squats, Swiss Ball Leg Curls, Hip Thrusts

Why:
- Calves are optional, so they are not in (section 10).
- Biceps and triceps get 1 isolation movement each, with the second work set handled by a compound: chinups for biceps, close grip bench press for triceps (section 7).
- Quads are one single leg movement (reverse lunge) plus one deep knee bend movement (goblet squat). Glutes and hamstrings are a leg curl plus a hip thrust (section 7).
