# 13. Tempo

- Move fast enough that the technique stays extremely strict. No free falls and no bouncing. There is no need to deliberately count slowly on the lowering part.
- The science: a 2025 meta-analysis of 14 studies (Enes et al., Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research) found tempo has minimal effect on muscle growth. An earlier review (Schoenfeld, Ogborn and Krieger, 2015, Sports Medicine) found lowering and lifting speeds from 0.5 to 8 seconds per rep gave similar growth, with reps over 10 seconds likely worse. So deliberately slowing the lowering phase is not needed for growth.
- There is another reason not to prescribe a lowering count. Lowering length adds a dimension to the lift that is logistically hard to normalize, which reduces the chance of an accurate PR signal. What matters most is the accuracy of the technique.
