# 20. Past injuries

What changes depends on why and how the injury happened. The overall decision principle: choose the exercise that runs the joint through the biggest range of motion with the least amount of discomfort.

Normal discomfort versus pain that means stop and swap the movement:
- Muscle soreness and muscle burning (lactic acid) are normal. Connective tissue pain and sharp impingement pain are not.
- During the exercise, they should not feel any sharp pinching, and no pain right where the muscle connects to the joint. They should feel it in the belly of the muscle.
- In the days after, the muscle should be stiff and sore, but only when they move. They should not feel it when they are not moving.

Knee pain: it comes from a combination of tight quads and hip flexors, very weak hips, and poor technique. In Dan's coaching experience, most people lack hip range of motion and hip strength, so the glutes and hip joint do not take their fair share of the load and the knee joint takes all of it. Over time that makes the quads very tight, which pulls on the patellar tendon and makes it worse. The solution: foam roll the quads a lot and stretch them with the rear foot elevated hip flexor stretch (one of the mobility drills). Then they need to learn to use their glutes and hips better and not cheat their leg motions. If the issue keeps coming back, the usual cause is how closely the technique and the mobility work are being followed. It is natural to want the reps at any cost rather than sink the hips into a full range of motion. Doing a leg movement correctly often shows a strength level that is significantly lower than expected, and that is a normal part of the process. Many people say they have strong legs but have never run them through more than a fraction of the range of motion.

Elbow tendonitis: it is always directly related to poor shoulder mechanics. Remove all the exercises where they feel it the most, and work on improving shoulder mechanics. Exercise selection shifts toward movements that make the shoulders move better, and away from movements that hurt the elbow. It takes many, many weeks to reverse. Elbow tendonitis builds up over many weeks of shoulder mechanics that are not working well, so it is slow to develop and slow to fix.

Leg day warmup: ATG split squats, maybe some foam rolling on the quads if they feel stiff, and mobility work if the person is tight. Upper body day warmup: nothing. Nothing is mandatory (see [protocol/training.md](../protocol/training.md)).
