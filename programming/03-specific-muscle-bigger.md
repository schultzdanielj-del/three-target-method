# 3. When the person wants a specific muscle bigger

- It is a subtle change, not an overhaul. Put the exercise that hits that muscle as early in the workout as possible, and/or choose movements for other muscle groups that overlap with it.
- Wanting a bigger muscle does not mean doing more sets on it. Do not add sets. A client who refuses the prescribed 2 working sets and does 10 or more keeps getting elbow pain. Short studies find more weekly sets grow muscle more over 8 to 12 weeks, with diminishing returns. TTM chooses the longest runway at the least sacrifice, so it does not add sets (see [criticisms.md](../objections/training-volume-failure-and-frequency.md)). Dan broke a plateau and added half an inch to his arms by dropping to just 2 work sets of biceps.
- Men get no default emphasis. Wanting a bigger upper body or bigger legs is everyone's goal.

Age (under 55) changes nothing in the plan. The program is auto-regulated: you set personal bests when the body is ready to do it. Biomechanics and mobility differ across all ages, and being younger does not mean someone performs exercises better. It depends on the lifter's individual experience and training age.
