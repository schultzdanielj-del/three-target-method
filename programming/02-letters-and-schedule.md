# 2. Letters and schedule

- Ideally each muscle (each letter) is hit every 8 to 10 days. 14 days is just the limit.
- 3 letters for 2 workouts a week, 4 letters for 3 workouts a week. 2 letters would mean 1 day a week, which is not enough frequency.
- If the person cannot do 2 workouts a week, the method is not for them. A one day plan could be made, but it would be a longer workout and would cap progress around late beginner or early intermediate. In Dan's coaching experience, people who can only give once a week almost never stick to once a week either, and no lowering of the bar creates adherence for them. Do not build a one day plan.
- Some people on a 4 letter plan end up training about 2 days a week. The plan calls for 3 days a week. In Dan's coaching experience, asking less does not fix this, and making the plan easier rarely increases adherence. Do not shrink the plan to match lower adherence.
- Grouping depends on the person. The usual 4 letter split is chest and shoulders, back, arms, legs. The usual 3 letter split is push, pull, legs.
- Example of a different split: a consistent, experienced woman with female physique goals gets more emphasis on lower body, shoulders and back (legs split over two letters, arms folded into the push and pull letters).
- Legs are more taxing on the body. Give leg letters fewer exercises (usually 4) where you can. Other letters have 4 to 6 exercises. With only 3 letters to fill all the required slots, 6 is sometimes not enough, so a letter with no lower body work (calves aside) can go over 6 (Dan). The site's sample 2 day plan has 7 on letter A. Never 7 on an ABCD (4 letter) plan, and never 7 on a letter with lower body work other than calves (Dan).
- It is hard to judge what a full effort is. When people look at a plan, it often feels like a lot, and the natural urge is to scale it back. This plan is the minimum that gets constant forward progress, and it cannot be reduced further. The reduction is already built in: no calorie counting, simple eating, freedom in the choices, and as little working out as possible. It is already as low as logic allows. Going lower would remove the results.
- If the person pushes to shorten or change the plan: the plan cannot be shortened. It can be added to, with weekly steps, non lifting activity, or additional mobility work (unlimited).
- They can also train muscle groups that are not trained yet, for example add calves if they are not in. They can add 2 light sets of external rotations or hip abductions ("sprinkles on top"). They cannot add more volume to existing muscle groups. The effect will be the opposite of what they expect.
- Requests to shorten the plan are usually driven by how the plan feels, not by what the schedule allows. In Dan's coaching experience, the reasons given for shortening are often well argued, and they still do not change what the plan needs to work. The method cannot do the work for the person, and consistency is what decides the result.
- This plan is already built for time limited people. Anything less is not sufficient. If a person wants less than this, that is their choice and a fair one, but do not shorten the plan; the method does not offer a smaller version.
