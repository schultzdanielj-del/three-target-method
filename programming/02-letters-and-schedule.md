# 2. Letters and schedule

- Ideally each muscle (each letter) is hit every 8 to 10 days. 14 days is just the limit.
- 3 letters for 2 workouts a week, 4 letters for 3 workouts a week. 2 letters would mean 1 day a week, which is not enough frequency.
- If the person cannot do 2 workouts a week, the method is not for them. A one day plan could be made, but it would be a longer workout and would cap progress around late beginner or early intermediate. The type of person who can only give once a week is almost never adherent to once a week either, and no lowering of the bar creates adherence for them. Do not build a one day plan.
- Some 4 letter clients end up training about 2 days a week. They should be training 3 days a week; they are just not adherent. Asking less of them does not fix that, and making it easier rarely increases adherence. Do not shrink the plan to match low adherence.
- Grouping depends on the person. The usual 4 letter split is chest and shoulders, back, arms, legs. The usual 3 letter split is push, pull, legs.
- Example of a different split: a highly adherent, higher skilled, more experienced woman with female physique goals gets more emphasis on lower body, shoulders and back (legs split over two letters, arms folded into the push and pull letters).
- Legs are more taxing on the body. Give leg letters fewer exercises (usually 4) where you can. Other letters have 4 to 6 exercises. With only 3 letters to fill all the required slots, 6 is sometimes not enough, so a letter with no lower body work (calves aside) can go over 6 (Dan). The site's sample 2 day plan has 7 on letter A. Never 7 on an ABCD (4 letter) plan, and never 7 on a letter with lower body work other than calves (Dan).
- People have no absolute frame of reference for what a full effort is, or what doing everything is. They take their current plan and emotionally try to scale it back. This plan is the bare minimum that will get constant forward progress, and it cannot be negotiated any lower. That was already done for them: they are not counting calories, they eat simple, they have freedom, and they work out as little as possible. It is already negotiated as low as logic allows in the best possible system. They will not feel that. To them it feels like a lot, so the urge will be to go even lower and box themselves out of any results.
- If the person pushes to shorten or change the plan: the plan cannot be shortened. It can be added to, with weekly steps, non lifting activity, or additional mobility work (unlimited).
- They can also train muscle groups that are not trained yet, for example add calves if they are not in. They can add 2 light sets of external rotations or hip abductions ("sprinkles on top"). They cannot add more volume to existing muscle groups. The effect will be the opposite of what they expect.
- The negotiation has nothing to do with reality. It is people emotionally running from accountability. People are smart, so the strategies they use are sophisticated: therapy language, unassailable statements, logical reasoning. All of it happens without them knowing, in pursuit of their own failure.
- This plan is already built for time limited people. Anything less is not sufficient. If a person wants less than this, they do not care enough about getting in shape, and that is okay, but do not shorten the plan and do not entertain the stories people tell.
