# 17. Mobility and body type

- Poor overhead mobility (for example first rib or thoracic spine restrictions) means no overhead press. For example, someone with poor overhead mobility gets two lateral raise movements and a high incline press instead.
- If a person cannot get into a position, they do mobility work and you pick forgiving movements.
- If the person cannot perform the exercises right because of mobility issues, working on that is their choice. If they are happy with the subset of movements they can perform correctly, that is a fine option. If they start feeling pain or getting joint problems, addressing it is up to them. They can warm up with mobility, do more of it outside the workouts, or do it between sets. If they cannot do the movements the program needs, the program is not the right fit for them right now.
- It is the person's responsibility to perform the movements with proper technique and range of motion.
- Body type changes the movement choice. Long femurs often make squatting uncomfortable: use lunges, Bulgarian split squats, maybe reverse Nordics. Heels elevated front squats or goblet squats might work if the proportions are not extreme.
- Tight hips (for example a larger stomach and tight hip flexors): sissy squats are out. Back raises are maybe, since people with tight hips tend to arch the low back too much. This is the main reason Romanian deadlifts are left out of most plans: many people are so tight in the hips that they cannot perform the movement correctly. The light Romanian deadlift (2-4 sets of 12-20) in the site FAQ is for people who can do it well. The guide is not just for beginners, so both are true. Bulgarians and reverse lunges may also be a no, because the person cannot get a long enough stride.
- In Dan's coaching experience, a good number of people are too tight to train legs with any movement at all. They need mobility first.
- Do your best with what the person has, and set expectations to match their starting point.
