# 17. Mobility and body type

- Poor overhead mobility (for example first rib or thoracic spine restrictions) means no overhead press. Example: one client gets two lateral raise movements and a high incline press instead.
- If a person cannot get into a position, they do mobility work and you pick forgiving movements.
- If the person cannot perform the exercises right because of mobility issues, they need to fix that, and it is their choice. If they are happy with the subset of movements they can perform correctly, so be it. If they start feeling hurt or getting joint problems, it is their responsibility to address it. They can warm up with mobility, do more of it outside the workouts, or do it between sets. If they are not qualified to do the program, it is what it is.
- It is the person's responsibility to perform the movements with proper technique and range of motion.
- Body type changes the movement choice. Long femurs will hate squatting: use lunges, Bulgarian split squats, maybe reverse Nordics. Heels elevated front squats or goblet squats might work if the proportions are not extreme.
- Tight hips (for example a big belly and tight hip flexors): sissy squats are out. Back raises are maybe, since these people tend to arch the low back too much. This is the main reason Romanian deadlifts are left out of most plans: many people are so tight in the hips that they butcher the movement. The light Romanian deadlift (2-4 sets of 12-20) in the site FAQ is for people who can do it well. The guide is not just for beginners, so both are true. Bulgarians and reverse lunges may also be a no, because the person cannot get a long enough stride.
- A good chunk of people are too tight to train legs with any movement at all. They need mobility first.
- Do your best with what the person has. Some people want the Mona Lisa and all they have is crayons.
