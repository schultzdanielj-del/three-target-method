# 7. Movements per muscle

Two movements per muscle group is the default, with these notes:

| Muscle | Movements | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Chest | 2 | Dips count as chest and triceps, but technique matters for the chest part. |
| Shoulders | 2 | A vertical press and a lateral raise. A high incline press is pretty much a vertical press. |
| Triceps | 2 | Pushups, dips and similar can count as the second triceps movement. |
| Biceps | 2 | Underhand grip pulling counts as biceps work (see overlap). |
| Back | 4 | Split into lats and upper back. |
| Quads | 2 | One single leg movement plus one deep knee bend movement. |
| Glutes and hamstrings | 2 | A leg curl plus a hip thrust. |
| Abs | 2 | What the person can do with the equipment they have, usually a static hold plus a dynamic movement. |
| Calves | optional | See [section 10](10-calves.md). |

Biceps and triceps can be 1 isolation movement, with the second work set handled by a compound (a pulling movement for biceps, a pressing movement for triceps).

A woman is assumed to have female physique goals (more emphasis on lower body, shoulders and back) unless you get to know her more. It is just a heuristic.

Why some plans have only 1 biceps and 1 triceps movement: underhand grip chinups hit biceps hard, and a 3 letter split can only fit so much, so overlap does the rest.

Glutes and hamstrings are two separate muscle groups. A person whose goals are glute and hamstring dominant gets the maximum slots filled: 2 direct movements for glutes and 2 direct movements for hamstrings (4 in total, for example a leg curl, a back raise, a hip thrust and a hip abduction).

If a woman wants a female physique, bias toward glutes, lateral hip and hamstrings first, and split them across letters so they are hit fresher and harder. Missing a set of chest or direct arm work is no big deal. Missing glute, lateral hip or hamstring work is.
