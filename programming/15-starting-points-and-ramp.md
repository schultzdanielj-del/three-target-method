# 15. Starting points and ramp

- First set on any movement: aim for 15-25 reps, log it, beat it. Most people ignore this because they still approach it with convention in their head. Tell them again.
- No ramp in. There are already the fewest sets possible, so a new client starts the full plan from the first week. As long as they set a decent baseline starting number, the first few workouts are auto-regulated: an inexperienced person's first work sets are not real work sets, because their body cannot yet show the muscle's maximum potential.
- The starting number: for a less experienced person it might be capped by their technical ability and execution ability. For an advanced person it is just what feels good.
- The biggest mistake people make is doing too many sets, and going too heavy, so the starting number lands at too low a rep range.
- Early weeks: when the reps pass the top of the range, add about 10% weight, erring toward rounding down.
- Returning lifters: it is their call, and it is different for each area of the body. A body area with a history of injury should start very conservatively and be built slowly. Areas with no history can start harder. Dan's own low back (injured 15 years ago) is why his lunges, hip thrusts and reverse hypers started very conservatively while sissy squats and glute ham raises did not.

Progression after the early weeks
- Add the smallest increment the person's weights allow in the gym. If the smallest jump is 5 lb, they will have to go all the way up toward 30 reps first, so that the 5 lb jump keeps them at the bottom end of the rep range.
- Sometimes the person has to alternate between the heavier and the lighter weight, attempting PRs every other workout on each one, and ratchet back and forth until they make forward progress.
- A PR can be set from a different weight. As long as the reps stay inside the rep range, it is completely valid. Example: if the best PR is at 20 lb, but a PR at 25 lb would still be inside the rep range, they can try 25 lb, or the original 20 lb for more reps, or 15 lb if that is also still inside the range. Work backwards from the score formula ((weight x reps x 0.0333) + weight) to figure out what a PR would be at a different weight.

Switching gyms or machines: not all machines are the same. On a different machine, set a new baseline (log it as a new first set to beat) instead of carrying over the old number.
