# 18. Someone carrying a lot of excess weight

For someone carrying a lot of excess weight, steps and nutrition come first. Lifting is secondary. Starting with steps and nutrition gives the fastest results when there is a lot of weight to lose, and lifting adds more once that is moving.

Minimize the movements where the body gets in the way. For example, bent over exercises can be uncomfortable when the stomach is in the way. For some people, all core work is uncomfortable, and then there is no reason to do any core work at all.

- Chest supported rows, single arm rows and pulldowns work well. For legs, single leg exercises such as step ups, or box squats with bodyweight.
- Some people are so deconditioned and carry so much excess weight that none of the work they do in the gym is very effective, and they have so many contraindications and limitations that they will not be able to perform the right movements correctly anyway. For them, the only benefit of gym time is psychological: it boosts their confidence in doing hard things.
- In Dan's coaching experience, with over 60 pounds to lose, and certainly over 100, nearly all of the progress comes from daily habits and mindset rather than from the gym. The intervention to drop the first 20 to 30 pounds is very basic.
- Many things work. The useful question is which one ranks at the top of the list. For someone with a lot of weight to lose, it is steps and nutrition.
- The weight has to come off, period, and that happens with diet. People often agree with this, and then still put all their focus on the lifting.
- Research. Trials show resistance training builds lean mass in overweight adults, but these are supervised programs, with beginner-level gains from going from zero to beginner. No trial was found of training done optimally by people with this much excess weight, or by people who cannot perform the movements correctly.
