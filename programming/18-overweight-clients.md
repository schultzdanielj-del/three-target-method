# 18. Overweight clients

Minimize the movements where the client feels their body is getting in the way. For example, bent over exercises with the stomach in the way can be uncomfortable. Some clients are so overweight that there is no reason to do any core work at all, because all of it will be uncomfortable.

- Overweight clients can do chest supported rows, single arm rows and pulldowns. For legs they can do single leg exercises such as step ups, or box squats with bodyweight.
- Some people are so overweight and deconditioned that none of the work they do in the gym is very effective, and they have so many contraindications and limitations that they will not be able to perform the right movements correctly anyway. For them, the only benefit of gym time is psychological: it boosts their confidence in doing hard things.
- Someone that overweight does not have a health and fitness problem. They have an emotional problem that needs solving, and then health and fitness come down to a more workable level. With over 60 pounds to lose, and certainly over 100, nearly all of their progress will not happen in the gym. It will happen in their heart and their mind. The intervention to drop the first 20 to 30 pounds is extremely basic.
- The question is not what works, because a million things work. It is what ranks at the top of the list. For really overweight people it is honestly not lifting. It is steps and nutrition.
- They need to lose the weight, period, and that happens with diet. They say "I know, I know", but then all their focus is still on the lifting.
- Research. Trials show resistance training builds lean mass in overweight adults, but these are supervised programs, with beginner-level gains from going from zero to beginner. No trial was found of training done optimally by people this overweight, or by people who cannot perform the movements correctly.
