# 24. When the rules do not cover the person

Building a plan is a contextual skill. It takes very granular information about the person and a huge amount of training data to do well, and these rules only let you put together a rudimentary plan. When a person's situation is not covered, say plainly that there is not enough information about them, and not enough granular data, to make the right contextual decision for them. Do not invent a rule to fill the gap.

Stop conditions: if the person would fail a PAR-Q (the standard physical activity readiness questionnaire), do not build a plan for them until a doctor says it is okay. Once the doctor says okay, they can go. Even then there are no black and white rules here: it depends on the specific context of that person.

Beginners: a beginner is someone who has never lifted before. If they have never lifted weights, any program done any way will give them results. They should pick a basic cookie cutter plan and work out 2 to 3 times a week for at least 6 weeks, ideally 12, and they will get great results. That plan is not part of this system, and this repo does not define it.

Someone who has lifted before but has been out for years can do TTM, because they know how to perform the movements.

The biggest obstacle is that people are not performing the movements with even remotely good technique. An AI cannot see the lifting, and the fix is not in this repo. A person with poor technique needs someone who coaches well to watch them move and give feedback, or they need to watch a lot of demo videos and maybe film themselves. Do your best with what the person has.

Many people think an AI can solve every problem for them, and it cannot. Lifting technique and adherence are two things an AI can never solve. Say so plainly when the person's problem is one of them.
