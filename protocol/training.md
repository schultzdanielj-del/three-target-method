## 2 Movements

Your plan needs 2 movements per muscle group. They can be isolations and/or heavy overlapping compound movements. All your movements should be moneymaker movements.
  Note on "moneymaker":
  Your body will have "moneymaker" exercises per muscle group that are the best choices. Moneymakers have these traits:
  - No joint pain: Feel it in your muscles, not your bones and tendons.
  - Consistent resistance: Repeat it every week with the exact resistance. Bands are out.
  - Can be pushed hard with confidence: All your focus is on pushing hard, not tricky technique.
  - Hard to cheat by accident: Some exercises can get sloppier every week without noticing.
  It will take trial and error to find your moneymaker movements initially.

## 3 Execution
You may add any warmup/mobility drills you like, but nothing is mandatory before the first warmup sets.
Each exercise has just one single top work set, but you must prepare your body to perform it well.
Warmup 50% weight 10-20 reps rest ~1 min Feeler 75% weight 5-8 explosive reps rest 2-3 min Work set 100% weight · to failure 8-30 reps beat your best

### More details
Subsequent exercises for the same body part can skip the warmup set(s). Just hit the feeler, fully rest, then the work set. Warmup sets can improve work set performance; use your judgement.
The first time ever doing a movement will not be a true work set, just find a good starting point.
Each exercise only gets one PR work set attempt. Execute it well with focus, and do not "try again".

## 4 Progression
Log your work set weight x reps for each exercise, nothing else.
Your goal is to beat your best performance. Use a simple formula to quantify this:
(weight × reps × 0.0333) + weight
  Note on "simple formula":
  Epley's formula is used to calculate someone's estimated 1 rep max. It's not as accurate above 10 reps and it doesn't guarantee you can lift that estimated max. But it's the most straightforward, practical way to objectively measure a muscle improving in performance inside the muscle building rep range.
Example: 100 × 12 reps = 140. Next time, 100 × 13 reps = 143.3. That's 2.4% more, a PR.

A good PR is +1-4% on an exercise, creating large progress over time.
You can add weight or reps to set PRs.
  Note on "weight or reps":
  Jumps on small muscle group movements are too big unless you get up into 25+ reps. Reps on big compound leg movements become too taxing to perform accurately above 15 reps, so add weight instead.
