# Example E (man, shoulder and elbow problems, weak upper back)

Profile: man with a very weak upper back, a shoulder problem, elbow tendonitis, low back issues, and weak glutes and hips with poor lockout technique. 4 letters.

Plan:
- A: Incline Barbell Bench Press, Flat DB Bench Press, Seated Lateral Raises, Lateral Raises, Weighted Planks
- B: DB Chest Supported Rows, Pulldowns, Seated Low Cable Rows, Single Arm DB Rows, Head Supported Rear Delt Flys
- C: Toe Press, Seated Dumbbell Curls, Cable Straight Bar Bicep Curl, Incline DB Triceps Extensions, Straight Bar Pushdowns
- D: Dumbbell Reverse Lunges, Leg Press Machine, Lying Machine Leg Curl, 45 Degree Back Raise

Why:
- The back letter has 5 back movements, not 4, because, in Dan's coaching experience, a very weak upper back commonly goes together with overdone chest and biceps, and because of the shoulder problem and elbow tendonitis ([section 8](../programming/08-choosing-the-movement.md), Back).
- The back raise is there because there is no reverse hyper available. Light back raises with proper technique get the hips moving more and the low back moving less. They are light for accuracy ([section 8](../programming/08-choosing-the-movement.md), Hinges).
- The lunges and the back raise are light sets (fixed sets and reps: 2x12-15 per side and 3x12-20). Low back issues, weak glutes and hips and poor lockout technique mean lots of glute lockout reps are needed ([section 8](../programming/08-choosing-the-movement.md), Glutes and hamstrings).
