# Example E (man, bad shoulder and elbow tendonitis)

Profile: man with the weakest upper back of the examples here, a bad shoulder, elbow tendonitis, low back issues, and weak glutes and hips with poor lockout technique. 4 letters.

Plan:
- A: Incline Barbell Bench Press, Flat DB Bench Press, Seated Lateral Raises, Lateral Raises, Weighted Planks
- B: DB Chest Supported Rows, Pulldowns, Seated Low Cable Rows, Single Arm DB Rows, Head Supported Rear Delt Flys
- C: Toe Press, Seated Dumbbell Curls, Cable Straight Bar Bicep Curl, Incline DB Triceps Extensions, Straight Bar Pushdowns
- D: Dumbbell Reverse Lunges, Leg Press Machine, Lying Machine Leg Curl, 45 Degree Back Raise

Why:
- The back letter has 5 back movements, not 4, because he has the weakest upper back and it goes together with overdone chest and biceps, plus the bad shoulder and elbow tendonitis ([section 8](../programming/08-choosing-the-movement.md), Back).
- The back raise is there because he has no reverse hyper. Light back raises with proper technique get the hips moving more and the low back moving less. They are light for accuracy ([section 8](../programming/08-choosing-the-movement.md), Hinges).
- The lunges and the back raise are light sets (fixed sets and reps: 2x12-15 per side and 3x12-20). His low back issues, weak glutes and hips and poor lockout technique mean he needs lots of glute lockout reps ([section 8](../programming/08-choosing-the-movement.md), Glutes and hamstrings).
